Build to Scale · 13

Six cost drivers determine what it costs to run AI at scale.

Understanding the cost driver tree enables accurate FinOps modelling and prevents surprises in the business case. Click each driver to expand its levers.

Inference compute
Model selection: smaller, quantised, or distilled models reduce cost per token significantly
Caching strategies: prompt caching for repeated context reduces redundant inference
Batching: asynchronous workloads can be batched vs. real-time premium pricing
Data retrieval and embedding
Embedding model size and refresh frequency drive storage and compute cost
Vector database sizing and query patterns determine retrieval infrastructure cost
Human review and oversight
Review rate determines staffing: what share of AI outputs require human sign-off?
Escalation design: clear confidence thresholds reduce unnecessary escalation volume
Model risk and compliance
Model validation cycles, documentation, and SR 11-7 equivalents drive ongoing cost
Bias monitoring, fairness testing, and explainability tooling are ongoing, not one-off
Platform and tooling
MLOps platform licensing or build cost amortised across use case volume
Feature store, model registry, and experiment tracking infrastructure
Retraining and drift management
Retraining frequency depends on regulatory change velocity and data drift rates
Automated drift detection reduces manual monitoring overhead over time
Cost driver taxonomy is indicative. Size each driver during Diagnose phase using client architecture, cloud provider, and transaction volumes.

Reference · A5

Lean client entry: methodology principles.

P1
Evidence before design
No target state is designed before the diagnostic evidence is assembled. Prevents designing for a context that doesn't match the client's actual operating model.
P2
Gate-first, not timeline-first
Each phase has a gate test. The next phase only begins when the gate test passes. Timelines are targets, not commitments.
P3
Lighthouse before platform
Prove value in one controlled use case before investing in platform infrastructure. Lighthouse evidence justifies the platform investment.
P4
Human-centred AI design
Every use case design specifies exactly which decisions remain with humans, which are AI-assisted, and what the escalation triggers are. Not configurable at runtime without governance approval.

Reference · A8

Responsible AI: minimum checklist for risk use cases.

Explainability
Every AI decision that affects a person or portfolio must produce an explanation that satisfies the relevant regulator's standards. Confirm this before selecting a model architecture.
Bias and fairness
Training data and model outputs must be audited for protected-class bias before deployment. Ongoing monitoring required at production.
Human oversight
Decision authority limits must be defined before pilot begins. AI does not exceed its defined authority without explicit governance approval.
Data provenance
All training and inference data must have a documented lineage, consent basis (where applicable), and retention policy aligned to regulatory requirements.
Model risk governance
All production models must be registered, documented, validated, and approved per the client's MRM policy before deployment. SR 11-7 equivalents apply in applicable jurisdictions.
