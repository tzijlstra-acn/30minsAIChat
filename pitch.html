Appendix · Optional and buyer-led

Four doors into the detail.

Open a door when a buyer asks. Each page answers one question and returns to the story. Keys: 1 to 4 open a module, A returns here, M returns to the story.

Where should we start?

With a bounded journey where the operating pain is real, the work is standardised enough and the outcome can be measured.

Where does value come from?

From better risk outcomes and faster, steadier operations, with capacity redirected to judgement. Headcount is not the base case.

What are the unit economics?

Cost per case falls when preparation removes addressable work, net of exceptions and run cost. Change the inputs; the model recalculates.

How does the business case improve with reuse?

The first journey pays for shared foundations. Later journeys cost less where reuse is real, but each keeps its own domain work.

What does total cost include?

Far more than model usage: redesign, data, connectors, assurance and ownership carry most of the cost.

What belongs to AI, rules and people?

AI prepares, deterministic rules constrain, and people hold every material judgement.

How is each capability governed?

In proportion to its authority: assistance drafts, apps act inside a task, automations run approved steps, ad hoc agents read and draft.

How does an ad hoc agent become managed capability?

Through gated stages, each with more ownership and review, and with retirement built in.

How does governed write-back work?

Every material action is proposed, previewed, checked, approved, executed by a governed connector and recorded.

How is the capability assured in operation?

With four layers of monitoring and named owners for incidents, pausing and fallback.

How does the Workspace connect without replacing systems?

It orchestrates work around the authoritative platforms. They keep the record; the Workspace reads, listens, ingests and writes back under control.

What are the integration patterns?

Four, each with its own latency and its own main control.

What context travels through the Workspace?

One case envelope, passed between the Workspace, role apps, analytics, agents and connectors, so every step shares the same scope.

Do we need a knowledge graph?

Only where the question depends on relationships across systems. For single records, metrics and workflow state, it adds nothing.

What would the NFR knowledge graph contain?

The entities NFR work already uses, joined by named relationships, each carrying its source and provenance.

How is the graph built safely?

Incrementally, for one journey at a time, with derived links treated as proposals until checked.

How does a question become a visual answer?

Governed data services calculate the facts; a validated insight schema chooses the chart; the model helps interpret and explain.

How are access and provenance preserved?

Identity flows from the Workspace to every query and action; results are filtered before the model or the screen sees them.

What sits behind the Workspace?

Four layers: the business experience, a runtime gateway, approved execution services and the authoritative systems.

What is reusable, and what is journey-specific?

Platform services and NFR capabilities are reused; process stages, evidence, thresholds and authority are designed per journey.

How do we prove one working loop in six months?

Three lanes move together and pass four gates; month six proves one complete loop on live or representative cases.

Who owns and operates it?

A named NFR product owner, with process, control, data and platform owners, and independent oversight kept separate.

How does it scale across the portfolio?

In waves: prove one loop, reuse it for adjacent journeys, build a domain portfolio, then use shared context across domains.
