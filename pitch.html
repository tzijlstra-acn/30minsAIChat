Appendix · Optional and buyer-led

Four doors into the detail.

Open a door when a buyer asks. Each page answers one question and returns to the story. Keys: 1 to 4 open a module, A returns here, M returns to the story.

Where should we start?

With a bounded journey where the value or risk significance is high and the data, method and ownership are ready enough to measure.

Where does value come from?

Better connected evidence leads to fewer late surprises, less rework and faster decisions, which return capacity to challenge and remediation.

What are the unit economics?

Cost per case falls when preparation removes addressable work, net of exceptions and run cost. Change the inputs; the model recalculates.

Why do later journeys cost less?

The first journey pays for shared foundations. Later journeys cost less where reuse is real, but each keeps its own domain work.

What does total cost include?

Far more than model usage: integration, data quality, method evaluation, change and assurance carry most of the cost.

Which capability handles which work?

Six questions decide the path: recurrence, systems, materiality, action, method stability and monitoring.

Why small methods, not one large model?

Each step does one job with a defined input, output and error rate, so it can be tested, challenged and reused.

Which methods exist?

A catalogue of method pools, each a small chain of steps assembled per question.

What does each analysis function do?

Five functions, each with allowed inputs, an expected output, a quality measure and a condition that escalates to a person.

How are methods evaluated and assured?

Each method is evaluated for its own task before use, then monitored in operation with named owners for incidents.

What belongs to AI, rules and people?

AI prepares, deterministic rules constrain, and named people hold every material judgement.

How does governed write-back work?

Every material action is resolved, checked, previewed, approved by name, executed by a governed connector, confirmed and recorded.

How does an ad hoc agent become managed capability?

Through gated stages, each with more ownership and review, with periodic revalidation and retirement built in.

What sits between the experience and the systems?

Four layers: the Workspace, a runtime gateway, approved execution services, and the authoritative systems that keep the record.

How do the systems connect?

Four patterns, each with its own latency, entitlement source, provenance and failure handling.

What context travels through the Workspace?

One context contract, shared by questions, the Work Hub, processes and agents, so every step works in the same scope.

What is the connected NFR core?

A relationship layer over the authoritative records: obligations, policies, risks, controls, evidence, assessments and decisions, linked with provenance.

When does a knowledge graph add value?

When the question depends on multi-hop relationships across systems. For lists, lookups and single-system work it adds nothing.

What would the graph model contain?

The entities NFR work already uses, joined by named relationships, each carrying its source, version and review status.

How is the graph built safely?

Incrementally, starting from the first journey, with derived links treated as proposals until reviewed.

How does a question become a visual answer?

Governed data services calculate the facts; a validated insight schema shapes the answer; the model helps interpret and explain.

How are entitlements, provenance and audit kept?

Identity flows to every query and action, read and write are separate permissions, and every answer and action carries its sources and versions.

What happens in the five-week qualification?

Three phases with one gate: baseline the question and data, configure and fit the methods, then run, challenge and decide.

What happens in the six months live?

Three layers move together through three phases and four gates, on real or production-representative cases.

What is reusable, and what is journey-specific?

The foundation and the NFR methods are reused and owned centrally; each journey designs its own mapping, rules, roles and actions.

Who owns and operates it?

Named accountabilities across the business, process, control, data, platform and model, with independent assurance kept separate.

How can we bring this in?

Three options on the same methods: a managed diagnostic, a dedicated managed environment, or a client deployment.

How does it scale across the portfolio?

From one question to a portfolio, one bounded step at a time, with reuse measured rather than assumed.
