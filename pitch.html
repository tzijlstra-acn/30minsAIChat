05 · Build to scale

The operating model determines
who owns what in an AI-assisted function.

First line — risk teams
Risk analyst
Reviews AI drafts; approves or escalates. Remains accountable for output quality.
Risk officer
Approves consequential decisions. Cannot delegate accountability to AI.
Domain SME
Validates AI output against specialist judgment. Flags calibration gaps.
Second line — oversight
Model risk manager
Validates AI models. Reviews scope, data, fairness, and drift monitoring.
Chief Compliance Officer
Confirms AI-assisted outputs meet regulatory and internal policy requirements.
Internal audit
Tests controls, samples AI decisions, verifies evidence completeness.
Technology and AI enablement
AI product owner
Owns use-case scope, success metrics, and adoption across risk teams.
Data steward
Maintains data lineage, quality, and access rights for AI ingestion.
Security and identity
Enforces data boundaries, prompt isolation, and model access controls.

REF · Role-impact methodology

How role-family split data was constructed

What the bars show
Each role family row shows the illustrative share of total risk FTE in each archetype (cantonal / private), and an indicative split of that headcount into: capacity freed by automation, both (augmented and automated), and output raised through AI-assisted judgment. These splits are directional — they are based on structured discussion with Accenture NFR practitioners, not client-validated time studies.
Universal / cantonal archetype (A)
Characterised by broad product range, large retail credit book, significant regulatory reporting burden, and embedded first-line risk teams across business units. Credit and underwriting (32%) dominates headcount. Data and reporting automation has high leverage.
Private / wealth archetype (B)
Characterised by high AUM concentration, complex client structures, strong regulatory and tax reporting requirements, and a proportionally larger non-financial risk function (35%) including conduct, operational risk, and fiduciary oversight. Model validation and appetite scenario work is relatively smaller.
Assumption and validation requirement
Before using any specific percentage in client communications, validate: (1) actual FTE distribution against the client's own HR or cost data; (2) time-per-task breakdown for priority roles; (3) current automation baseline (some roles may already have partial automation). Do not present these figures as Accenture-guaranteed benchmarks.

REF · Proof KPI library

What to measure in a proof of concept

Capacity and speed
  • Hours per regulatory change impact assessment (before vs after)
  • Time from RCSA trigger to draft sign-off
  • Analyst hours per monthly risk report cycle
  • Evidence assembly time per audit request
Risk and control quality
  • Exception catch-rate vs manual baseline (sample test)
  • False-negative rate for material risk events
  • Consistency score across assessors (AI-assisted vs manual)
  • Control test sample coverage rate
Regulatory and audit readiness
  • Evidence completeness score per exam request
  • Days from regulatory query to evidence package delivered
  • Model documentation completeness at submission
  • Number of audit findings related to evidence gaps
AI and model quality (process metrics)
  • Human override rate on AI draft outputs
  • Model confidence score distribution (calibration check)
  • Data freshness and completeness at ingestion
  • Hallucination or factual error rate (sampled review)

Do not present KPI targets without a validated client baseline. Use these as measurement framework, not promised outcomes.

REF · Responsible AI

Responsible AI and control checklist

Accountability and governance
  • A named human is accountable for every consequential AI-assisted decision
  • AI cannot approve its own outputs — a human review step exists
  • Scope of AI authority is documented and bounded
  • Escalation path defined for edge cases and failures
Data and model quality
  • Training and retrieval data is documented, sourced, and fit for purpose
  • Model validation has been completed (or is in scope)
  • Drift monitoring is in place with defined thresholds
  • Data lineage is traceable end to end
Fairness and bias
  • Potential protected-characteristic bias has been assessed for each use case
  • Outputs have been tested across relevant population segments
  • Adverse outcome review process exists
Transparency and explainability
  • AI-assisted outputs are labelled as such to human reviewers
  • Reasoning or evidence basis is surfaced alongside output
  • Confidence scores or uncertainty signals are provided where material
Security and privacy
  • Client and counterparty data is not used to train external models
  • Prompt isolation prevents data leakage between clients or sessions
  • Access controls and role-based permissions are enforced at the model layer
  • Data residency requirements are met
Regulatory alignment
  • Use case is reviewed against EU AI Act risk classification
  • Model risk management framework covers AI models in scope
  • Evidence of control effectiveness is available for regulatory review
REF · Architecture

Architecture principles and technology notes

Deployment model
Preference is sovereign cloud or on-premises deployment for all regulated risk data. LLM API calls should never send client transaction data or personal information to external model providers without explicit data-processing agreements and regulatory approval. Use retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to avoid embedding sensitive data in model weights.
Integration approach
Prioritise event-driven or API integration over batch extraction. Risk data freshness is a material concern — stale data embedded in AI context produces confident but wrong outputs. Data contracts between source systems and the AI layer should be version-controlled and tested.
Human-in-the-loop pattern
Every consequential output (risk assessment, regulatory submission, model decision, credit recommendation) must pass through a documented human review step before action. The AI layer surfaces output with confidence score and evidence provenance; the human reviewer approves, modifies, or rejects. The approval event is written to an immutable audit log.
Technology choices
Technology stack is client-driven — Accenture is vendor-neutral. Common patterns include Azure OpenAI (for European data residency), Databricks for ML and data pipelines, ServiceNow or Archer for GRC integration, and Confluent or Azure Event Hubs for event streaming. Any specific technology recommendation must be validated against client existing architecture and procurement constraints.
Scope of this slide deck
Architecture in this deck is illustrative — representative of common patterns observed across European bank engagements. It is not a delivered architecture design. A formal architecture assessment would require access to client system inventory, data flows, security policy, and cloud strategy.
