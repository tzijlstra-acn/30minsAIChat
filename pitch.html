01 — Setting the Scene

Three regulations. One deadline. Zero margin for error.

For a DACH bank, 2025 is not a normal year.

DORA
In force: 17 January 2025

EU Regulation 2022/2554 mandates ICT risk management frameworks, 4-hour initial incident notification to BaFin/FMA, ICT third-party registers, and Threat-Led Penetration Testing (TLPT) for significant institutions.

Impacts: BCM · TPRM · IS Risk · Incident Management
EU AI Act
High-risk AI: August 2026

Regulation 2024/1689 classifies credit scoring, fraud detection, AML screening and model-based risk decisions as high-risk AI. Conformity assessments, human oversight and model documentation are mandatory before deployment.

Impacts: MRM · Compliance · AI Governance
Basel IV / CRR III
In force: 1 January 2025

CRR III replaces the Advanced Measurement Approach with the Standardised Measurement Approach for operational risk capital. A 10-year internal loss database and the Internal Loss Multiplier are now mandatory inputs.

Impacts: ORM · RCSA · Capital Management
At the same time, MaRisk's 8th amendment (December 2022), the updated BAIT requirements, EMIR Refit, PSD3 and the incoming AML Regulation are placing simultaneous demands on your risk and compliance function. The question is not whether to act — it is how fast.
02 — The Problem

Your risk team is spending time on the wrong things.

Manual processes, fragmented data, and regulatory change velocity are creating an invisible cost.

RCSA and TPRM still run on spreadsheets.

Most banks complete their Risk and Control Self-Assessment annually — a process that takes months, involves dozens of workshops, and produces a snapshot that is out of date by the time it is approved. Third-party due diligence is largely manual, with analysts reading ISO 27001 certificates and ISAE 3402 reports line by line.

No one can read it all.

BaFin, EBA, ESMA, the ECB, the EU legislator and national transpositions are producing regulatory output at a pace that no compliance team can absorb manually. Horizon scanning is either reactive or missed entirely. Most banks discover material obligations weeks after publication — too late for an orderly implementation.

Eight NFR processes, eight separate spreadsheets.

TPRM, BCM, RCSA, ORM, MRM, Compliance, PKS and IS Risk each run their own data, their own tools, and their own reporting cycles. A vendor incident picked up by TPRM does not automatically trigger an ORM loss entry or a BCM review. The connections exist on paper — not in practice.

Everyone is implementing AI. No one is governing it.

Shadow AI is spreading across the first line. Risk models are being retrained with LLM-generated features. Procurement teams are signing AI vendor contracts without IT security review. The EU AI Act requires a governed AI inventory — most banks do not have one.
