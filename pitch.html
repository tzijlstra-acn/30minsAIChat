AI Landscape · 01

Three pressures are reshaping the risk agenda.

More obligations and evidence
Volume, complexity, and cross-border reach of requirements are compounding faster than manual compliance cycles can absorb.
More capable AI, with new controls
Client-facing, trading, and operations functions are deploying AI. The risk function may need to govern, audit, and approve at a comparable pace.
Pressure on capacity and speed
Capacity-based scaling may become a constraint. The cost per risk decision may need to fall while quality and explainability requirements rise.
Working hypothesis
The question is where the operating model may become a constraint.

Reference · A5

Lean client entry: methodology principles.

P1
Evidence before design
No target state is designed before the diagnostic evidence is assembled. Prevents designing for a context that doesn't match the client's actual operating model.
P2
Gate-first, not timeline-first
Each phase has a gate test. The next phase only begins when the gate test passes. Timelines are targets, not commitments.
P3
Lighthouse before platform
Prove value in one controlled use case before investing in platform infrastructure. Lighthouse evidence justifies the platform investment.
P4
Human-centred AI design
Every use case design specifies exactly which decisions remain with humans, which are AI-assisted, and what the escalation triggers are. Not configurable at runtime without governance approval.

Reference · A8

Responsible AI: minimum checklist for risk use cases.

Explainability
Every AI decision that affects a person or portfolio must produce an explanation that satisfies the relevant regulator's standards. Confirm this before selecting a model architecture.
Bias and fairness
Training data and model outputs must be audited for protected-class bias before deployment. Ongoing monitoring required at production.
Human oversight
Decision authority limits must be defined before pilot begins. AI does not exceed its defined authority without explicit governance approval.
Data provenance
All training and inference data must have a documented lineage, consent basis (where applicable), and retention policy aligned to regulatory requirements.
Model risk governance
All production models should be registered, documented, validated, and approved per the client's applicable model risk policy and supervisory expectations. Jurisdiction-specific requirements should be confirmed with Accenture's regulatory advisory practice.
